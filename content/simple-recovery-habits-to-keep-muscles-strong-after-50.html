Now Section 1 heading: "Prioritize Protein Timing and Quality"

Paragraph 1: Protein provides the amino acids muscles need to repair microscopic damage that occurs during exercise or even routine movement. After 50, the body’s ability to use protein efficiently can decline, so spreading intake across the day helps ensure a steady supply of building blocks. Aim for a source of protein at each meal—such as eggs, Greek yogurt, beans, fish, or lean poultry—and consider a modest snack that includes protein if you have a long gap between meals.

Paragraph 2: Research shows that consuming about 20 to 30 grams of protein within an hour after activity can support the repair process, but the total daily amount matters most. For most adults over 50, a target of 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day is a reasonable starting point. Pairing protein with carbohydrates—like a banana with nut butter or whole‑grain toast with cottage cheese—can further aid replenishment of energy stores while the amino acids get to work.

Section 2 heading: "Incorporate Gentle Movement and Stretching"

Paragraph 1: Light activity on recovery days promotes circulation, which helps deliver oxygen and nutrients to tired muscles while flushing out metabolic byproducts. Activities such as walking, swimming, or a slow bike ride keep the joints moving without adding strain. Paragraph 2: Stretching and mobility work complement gentle movement by maintaining flexibility and reducing tension. Simple routines—like calf stretches, shoulder rolls, or a seated spinal twist—can be done while watching TV or after a shower. Foam rolling or using a massage ball on tight areas for 30‑60 seconds per spot can also ease discomfort, but it’s best to keep pressure moderate and avoid rolling directly over bones or joints.
